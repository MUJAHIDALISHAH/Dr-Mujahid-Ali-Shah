Research: how the gut repairs itself

The intestine is lined by a single sheet of epithelium, wired by its own nervous system and patrolled by immune cells. When that lining is damaged, these systems must work together to restore it. Zebrafish larvae let me follow all three in a living, intact animal.

Epithelial injury and regeneration

Diet, microbes and repair

How diet and gut microbiota influence immune cells and the enteric nervous system during intestinal epithelial injury and regeneration, combining larval and adult zebrafish with transgenic reporter lines.

Enteric nervous system

Enteric neuron ablation and regeneration

A chemogenetic nitroreductase / metronidazole (NTR/MTZ) model to remove enteric neurons in larvae and follow how they come back. Next step: single-cell RNA sequencing of the regenerative process.

Inflammation

Dietary fat and enterocolitis

A fat-rich egg-yolk diet combined with DSS-induced enterocolitis, tracing how dietary fat reshapes the microbiota and its metabolites, and how that changes inflammation and recovery.

Neuroimmune communication

Nerves and immune cells under a high-fat diet

How dietary factors and inflammation affect neuronal and immune responses during repair: neuronal integrity, calcium imaging of enteric neurons, immune-cell dynamics and transcriptomics.

Approach

One question runs through the projects: what links what an animal eats to how well its gut recovers?

Diet→Injury or inflammation→Microbiota→Metabolites→Immune and neuronal response→Recovery

The work spans intestinal inflammation, microbiology, immunology and neuroscience.

  • ModelZebrafish (Danio rerio) larvae and adults, transgenic and reporter lines, in-house husbandry
  • ImagingLive light-sheet microscopy and Leica Thunder widefield imaging; calcium imaging of enteric neurons
  • Injury modelsChemogenetic NTR/MTZ cell ablation; DSS-induced enterocolitis
  • DietFat-rich egg-yolk feeding and dietary lipid manipulation
  • MolecularMolecular biology, transcriptomics and single-cell RNA sequencing
  • PhysiologyBehavioural assays in larvae
